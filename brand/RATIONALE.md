# Kobci logo concepts

These directions start from Kobci's practical role: it is the place where a small-business owner keeps sales, stock, customer debt, and daily cash. Every mark is a self-contained, hand-authored `32 × 32` SVG using only `currentColor`. None uses a gradient, font, image, external reference, or opacity.

## Collision audit

Before choosing any direction, each was checked against familiar small-icon categories. Three ascending bars were excluded because they read as mobile signal strength. Concentric arcs were excluded because they read as Wi-Fi. Battery outlines, triangular wedges, and isolated diagonals were excluded because they read as battery, play, cursor, or trend-arrow icons. Leaves, sprouts, rockets, and bare upward arrows were excluded as generic growth/startup clichés. Circular badges were also avoided because they can suggest a coin or crypto token.

## Keystone

**Idea:** A purpose-drawn, solid K makes Kobci itself the symbol, with a wide upright and two decisive diagonals that feel stable rather than decorative.

**Could be mistaken for:** A generic K monogram, a type specimen, or—without the wordmark—another company whose name begins with K. It does not resemble Wi-Fi, signal bars, a battery, play button, cursor, chart app, or any single famous logo closely, but all monograms have a general collision risk.

**At 16px:** Pass. The narrowest positive feature is 6 viewBox units (3 rendered pixels), the counters stay open, and there are no strokes to fall between pixels. The silhouette remains recognisably K-shaped in both teal-on-light and teal-on-dark.

**Where it fails:** It says "Kobci" more strongly than it says "business management." If the brand name is absent and the audience has never seen the mark, the meaning is not self-explanatory.

## Ledger

**Idea:** Two sturdy pages meet at a central gutter, representing one dependable record of the business.

**Could be mistaken for:** An open book, education product, butterfly, or wings. It avoids document-page corners that could make it look like a file-type icon, but the book association is unavoidable.

**At 16px:** Pass. The 3.6-unit gutter renders at roughly 1.8px and stays visibly open; the pages have broad, uncomplicated silhouettes with no ledger lines to fill in.

**Where it fails:** It can over-emphasise bookkeeping or learning and under-represent selling, stock movement, and production. It is also less ownable than a letterform.

## Trade Link

**Idea:** Two interlocking rounded links stand for sales, stock, debt, and cash staying connected through daily trade.

**Could be mistaken for:** Chain links, an infinity symbol, collaboration, or a generic hyperlink icon. Because it has no arrowheads, it avoids the sync/transfer-icon reading, but that reading is still possible at a glance.

**At 16px:** Conditional pass. The 4-unit stroke becomes 2px and remains clear, but the central overlap becomes a compact dark joint; it reads as a link before it reads as Kobci.

**Where it fails:** On rough photocopies or low-quality receipt printers the overlap can fill into a blob. It also has the weakest name recognition of the five.

## Inventory K

**Idea:** Six stock-like units form a pixel-built K, joining the company initial to the everyday reality of counted inventory.

**Could be mistaken for:** A QR fragment, Tetris piece, warehouse tiles, or a grid/dashboard app. It is deliberately not arranged as ascending bars, so it does not resemble mobile signal strength.

**At 16px:** Discard. Each unit renders cleanly at 4px and the gaps do not fill, but the intended K becomes unreliable: at exact 1× raster size it can read as a sparse grid or H. The geometry survives while the idea does not.

**Where it fails:** The modular/pixel character can feel more playful or software-startup-like than the desired plain, established tone. At tiny favicon size, a viewer may see six blocks before seeing the K.

## Account Seal

**Idea:** A K cut cleanly from a rounded square suggests an owner-controlled account, a dependable system boundary, and a mark that can be stamped.

**Could be mistaken for:** An app tile, bank/security product, or a generic K badge. The square rather than a circle avoids most coin/crypto readings, but a fintech association is still possible.

**At 16px:** Pass. The negative K has features of about 2–3 rendered pixels and stays open; the solid outer field survives reversal, receipt printing, and photocopying especially well.

**Where it fails:** The enclosure makes it heavier than the other concepts and slightly more "app icon" than "business identity." At very small sizes the rounded corners become visually square.

## Lockup proposal

Use the exact system stack `system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, Arial, sans-serif` at weight 700 with the spelling **Kobci**, never all caps in the primary lockup.

- **Horizontal:** mark at 32 units high; wordmark cap height approximately 19–20 units; gap 9 units. Centre the wordmark optically against the mark.
- **Stacked:** mark at 40 units high; wordmark at 24px; gap 8px. Centre both elements and reserve this arrangement for square or narrow placements.
- **Colour:** the whole lockup may inherit a single colour. On the current site that can be `#0d7a6b` on light surfaces or `#3ec9b0` on dark surfaces. The mark must also be usable in plain black or white for receipts, signs, and photocopies.
- **Clear space:** keep at least one quarter of the mark width around an icon-only use and at least one mark-stem width around either lockup.

A paid typeface is not necessary to launch. Later, a carefully licensed humanist sans could buy a more distinctive lowercase `b`, `c`, and `i`, plus more consistent wordmark metrics across platforms. It would not fix a weak mark, and the system stack remains the correct dependency-free default.

## Recommendation

**Recommend Keystone.** It is the plainest and most competent direction, has the cleanest 16px silhouette, avoids the growth and connectivity clichés, and builds recognition around the name rather than a generic software metaphor. Its lack of pictorial explanation is an acceptable trade: the wordmark supplies the name during adoption, while the K can become shorthand later.

Discard **Trade Link** because the centre is least reliable on rough printing and the symbol is too generic. Discard **Inventory K** because its pixel-block character is more playful than the positioning allows. Keep **Ledger** and **Account Seal** as secondary finalists if the owner wants, respectively, a more literal or a heavier institutional direction.

## Focused K-arrow study

This study follows the owner's direction that the K itself carries the growth. All four executions are hand-authored, one-colour `32 x 32` marks using `currentColor`. A visual sanity check found that K-plus-growth-arrow is a crowded stock-logo category, especially in finance and marketing, so restraint matters more than novelty by addition. The collision notes below are design checks, not trademark clearance.

### K-arrow Capline

**Idea:** The upper arm of a solid K ends in a filled rising arrowhead that stays inside the letter's cap height.

**Could be mistaken for:** The K remains dominant, so it does not read as a check mark; the filled tip has some play-button or cursor risk only when cropped away from the letter. The lower arm prevents an R or A reading. It is not a standalone next chevron. Its main famous-logo risk is the broad class of geometric K monograms and stock K-growth marks, rather than a close echo of a particular script, rounded, or split K logo.

**At 16px:** Pass; K-first: yes. The stem and lower leg remain broad, while the integrated head retains a visible point on light and dark at exact 1x raster size. No internal gap has to survive, so receipt-printer and photocopy behaviour is the strongest of the four.

**Where it fails:** At larger sizes the filled triangular tip makes the growth metaphor more explicit and therefore more generic. If reproduced with excessive ink spread, the small shoulder below the arrowhead can soften into a plain diagonal.

### K-arrow Ascender

**Idea:** A solid K sends the same filled upper-arm arrow above the cap height, giving the growth motion more energy and vertical reach.

**Could be mistaken for:** The high tip increases cursor and generic finance-arrow associations; if the vertical stem were hidden, the remaining diagonals could suggest a check mark. The full silhouette does not read as a play button, R, A, or next chevron. As with Capline, the meaningful famous-logo collision is the crowded category of angular K-arrow stock marks, not a specific script or rounded K.

**At 16px:** Pass; K-first: yes. The arrow tip remains present rather than clipping or vanishing, and the K stem/lower arm still carry more visual mass. The 1-viewBox-unit top margin is tight but survives in the tested raster.

**Where it fails:** The overshooting arrow dominates more at 32px and 64px and makes the lockup feel more like a startup, investment, or charting product. It also creates less even visual alignment beside the wordmark.

### K-arrow Crosscut

**Idea:** A separate filled arrow crosses the solid stem and becomes the K's upper diagonal, making the growth action visibly independent from the letter body.

**Could be mistaken for:** The short tail to the left of the stem is the execution most likely to suggest a check mark; the small filled head can resemble a cursor or play wedge. The complete stem and lower diagonal prevent R and A readings, and the mark is not a standalone next chevron. It also sits closest to common stock K-finance logos in which a contrasting arrow cuts across a letter, even though this version is deliberately one colour.
**Explicit famous-logo check:** This is the variant closest to the generic K-finance stock-logo category; no similarity check is trademark clearance.

**At 16px:** Conditional pass; K-first: yes. The K and the arrow point stay legible, but the separate crossing tail compresses to roughly a one-pixel cue, so the conceptual distinction from an integrated arm is weak at favicon size.

**Where it fails:** Ink spread can absorb the left tail into the stem, while a clean large reproduction can make that same tail look like an accidental spur. This is the least ownable and most collision-prone of the four.

### K-arrow Chevron

**Idea:** A 4-unit stroked K uses two square-ended strokes for the arrowhead instead of a filled triangle.

**Could be mistaken for:** The open head has the strongest next-chevron association and can look like a corner or expand control. It has little play-button or cursor resemblance because there is no filled wedge; the full stem rules out a check mark, R, or A. Its monoline construction shares a general geometric-K category with famous technology marks but does not depend on their distinctive rounded, script, or split-letter treatments.
**Explicit next chevron and famous-logo check:** The open corner can read as a next chevron; its wider collision class is the monoline geometric K, and no similarity check is trademark clearance.

**At 16px:** Pass; K-first: yes. The nominal stroke becomes 2px, the upper-arm point remains open, and the K is readable on both surfaces. The arrow reads second as a squared open head rather than disappearing.

**Where it fails:** A rough receipt printer can close the open corner or make the separate head look like a square hook. The stroked construction feels lighter and more interface-like than the solid variants, so it is less suitable for a shop sign or stamp.

### Recommendation among the four

**Recommend K-arrow Capline.** It preserves Keystone's solid, competent K silhouette, keeps the growth cue secondary, passes the 16px gate with the most printing tolerance, and avoids the extra startup energy of an overshooting or cross-letter arrow.

Keep **K-arrow Ascender** only if the owner wants more visible motion at larger sizes. Keep **K-arrow Chevron** as the lighter alternative, with the understood receipt-printing risk. Do not advance **K-arrow Crosscut**: it is only a conditional 16px pass and falls closest to generic stock K-finance marks.

## Fluid K-sweep throat study

This study holds the heavy 7-unit stem, lower leg, outer sweep trajectory, and arrow endpoint constant. Only the inner sweep boundary changes. The measured counter is the narrowest open horizontal throat from the stem's right edge at `x=11` to the inner sweep boundary immediately before the intentional structural join. At a 16px render, one viewBox unit equals 0.5 device pixels.

### K-sweep Tight

**Idea:** A heavy K carries a strongly thickening brush-like sweep whose inner boundary leaves the smallest possible counter before joining the stem.

**Could be mistaken for:** The packed curved centre has the highest camera aperture risk because the stem, lower leg, and sweep compress into three dark lobes. The pointed tip can read as a cursor/pointer, while the upper curve can read as a swoosh (Nike territory) or a generic motion sports mark when separated from the wordmark. If the stem is lost in poor reproduction, the remaining diagonals can suggest a check mark. The full silhouette still reads as K first.

**At 16px:** Fail; K-first: yes, arrow-second: yes. The narrowest counter is **1.5 viewBox units**, which scales to **0.75 device pixels at 16px**. That produces antialiasing but no stable full background pixel, so the throat can bleed shut on screen and is not defensible on a receipt printer or photocopy.

**Where it fails:** The centre turns into a dense knot under ink spread, making the tuning look less like a confident K and more like a blob with a pointed upper edge. Its extra head weight does not compensate for the lost counter.

### K-sweep Medium

**Idea:** The same K opens the counter and moderates the sweep's thickening so the curved arm remains fluid without becoming visually detached.

**Could be mistaken for:** The curve still enters swoosh (Nike) and generic motion sports mark territory, although the heavy stem keeps it from becoming a standalone swoosh. The point retains some cursor/pointer resemblance. The more open centre reduces camera aperture risk, and the complete stem prevents the whole mark from reading as a check mark.

**At 16px:** Conditional pass; K-first: yes, arrow-second: yes. The narrowest counter is **3.0 viewBox units**, which scales to **1.5 device pixels at 16px**. The raster shows about one full background pixel plus antialiasing: enough for a clean display, but still vulnerable to receipt-printer spread and repeated photocopying.

**Where it fails:** Its screen result is acceptable while its physical reproduction margin remains thin. At larger sizes the sweep is elegant, but the mark can feel more like a sports or logistics identity than plain business-management software.

### K-sweep Open

**Idea:** The same K uses the widest counter and the least head swelling, giving the curved sweep enough air to remain separate from the stem at favicon size.

**Could be mistaken for:** This is the clearest K and has the lowest camera aperture risk, but its longer, cleaner curve is also the closest of the three to a swoosh (Nike territory) and a generic motion sports mark. The pointed end can still suggest a cursor/pointer. Without the stem the two diagonals could resemble a check mark, but the intact 7-unit stem keeps the K reading primary.

**At 16px:** Pass; K-first: yes, arrow-second: yes. The narrowest counter is **4.5 viewBox units**, which scales to **2.25 device pixels at 16px**. Roughly two full background pixels remain before antialiasing, so the counter survives light and dark raster tests and has the only credible receipt/photocopy margin of the three.

**Where it fails:** Opening the counter makes the sweep feel more independent and therefore more swoosh-like. At 64px the flowing upper arm can imply sports, speed, or logistics more strongly than accounting, stock, and daily cash.

### Recommendation among the three

**Recommend K-sweep Open.** It is the only fluid tuning with a counter wide enough to pass the 16px and practical print gate; Tight fails, and Medium does not leave enough physical-production margin.

### Fluid versus K-arrow Capline

The fluid Open mark is more expressive and closer to the owner's visual preference, but it does **not** earn its extra risk for Kobci's required use cases. It introduces a real swoosh/motion-sports collision, weakens the arrow reading at 16px, and depends on a negative-space throat that Capline does not need. **I would ship K-arrow Capline.** If the owner explicitly chooses fluidity over maximum reliability, K-sweep Open is the only tuning I would advance.
